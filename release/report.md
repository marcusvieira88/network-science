## Did custodians leave during the crisis? (H26, post hoc)

Stated in docs/hypotheses-exploratory.md (O1) after every other result and before it was computed. A custodian at risk at a window's start (sending and receiving in at least 4 of the 16 weeks before, and not already silent) **departs** when they send nothing for 16 weeks while, in at least 4 of those weeks, their mailbox receives mail that only it can hold (from a non-custodian, to no other custodian). If that mail stops too, the silence is a **record end**: the mailbox was no longer collected, which is not a departure. Weeks are read from the presence of mail, not from hours or volumes. The crisis window is compared with the same weeks a year earlier (one-sided Fisher exact test), with a pre-crisis placebo, with every earlier window of the same length, leaving each custodian out, and under the robustness variants. Counts below k are suppressed; a p-value is shown only when every count it comes from reaches k, otherwise its band.

Custodians who stopped sending for 16 weeks or more while their own mailbox kept receiving mail: fewer than 5 of 66 at risk in the crisis window, against fewer than 5 of 26 at risk in the same weeks a year earlier (one-sided Fisher p 0.05 or above). Record ends (the mailbox stops holding mail too): fewer than 5 in the crisis window, fewer than 5 in the reference window. The crisis share is not above every one of the 55 earlier windows of the same length with at least 20 custodians at risk; the pre-crisis placebo has p 0.05 or above; leave-one-out does not apply, since the test itself does not pass. Of the 9 robustness variants in the rule, 2 keep the crisis share above the reference share and 0 keep p below 0.05; 1 publishes no comparison, because a window has too few custodians at risk. Counting every silence, record ends included (variant any silence), the crisis share is still not significantly above a year earlier. Power: even with no departure a year earlier, the crisis window would have needed at least 9 departures of 66 at risk for significance, so the test can see only a large wave; the placebo would have needed 16 of 51, so its passing shows little. No departure wave can be established: fewer than 5 custodians departed in the crisis window; the crisis share is not significantly above the same weeks a year earlier (p 0.05 or above); an earlier window of the same length had at least as high a share. Split at 2001-10-16 (third-quarter loss announced), at least one part holds fewer than 5 departures, so neither is published.

## The finding

No finding about the crisis year is claimed. H2, H3 passed the pre-registered statistical tests but failed its check against a known artifact of the data (release/artifact_checks.csv): the apparent change is explained by how the data was collected and analysed, not by a detectable change in how people communicated. Every outcome is listed in the report.

## Privacy and re-identification risk

Every published table meets a minimum group size of k = 5. The risk of the person-level output R0 was tested by attacking it, not asserted. R1 and R2 hold no person-level records, so both person-level attacks return no guesses by construction: their re-identification rate of zero restates that design, it is not a measurement of their risk.

Releases attacked:

- **R0**: pseudonymized person-level weekly edge list (the naive output; never published)
- **R1**: aggregates only, every cell meeting the minimum group size, bucketed degrees, weekly resolution; it contains no pseudonyms, and groups carry size-ordered labels
- **R2**: R1 plus seeded Laplace noise of a fixed scale on degree counts and group tie weights; the noise is not calibrated to any privacy budget (a sketch, not differential privacy)

Attackers:

- **A1**: outsider who holds the public corpus and matches per-address activity features
- **A2**: insider who knows a few true identities and propagates from them through the graph
- **A3**: the same propagation seeded with every custodian: the public corpus names every mailbox owner, so anyone holding it knows them all

## Limits

- Coverage: the corpus holds a limited set of mailboxes, mostly senior staff. A tie is visible only when one end is a collected mailbox, so results describe this sample, not the whole organization.
- Retention and selection: what people kept and what was collected differ by person and period, and the collection was shaped by an investigation.
- Headers only: subjects are content and are excluded, so there are no threads and no reply times. Deduplication cannot use content and merges distinct messages from one sender to the same recipients in the same second.
- Identity resolution is rule-based and favours precision; aliases it misses split one person into several nodes.
- Pseudonymization is not anonymization: person-level pseudonymized data must be treated as re-identifiable (see the attack on R0), so only aggregates are published; the noise variant is a sketch, not a formal privacy budget.
- Observational design: a structural change near public events does not show what people knew. Hypotheses fixed in advance limit hindsight bias but do not remove it.
- No group-level attack on the community graph was run: an attacker who knows some group members could try to infer ties or membership from group-to-group weights, and that risk is bounded only by the minimum of k distinct senders per published tie, not measured.
- The effect interval is computed at a change week chosen from the same data (the week that maximises the change statistic), so it is optimistic: it ignores the uncertainty of where the change happened.
- For H5–H25, the pre-crisis placebos run on fewer custodians than the crisis comparison (fewer meet the minimum message count in the January-to-April windows; see the placebo custodians column), so they have less power: a passing placebo does not exclude a pre-existing trend. Both placebo windows also lie before the change of the timestamp convention (see the record section), so the placebo cannot detect an artifact of it.
- The permutation p-values of H1–H4 treat weeks as exchangeable; weekly network metrics are autocorrelated, and a calibration test in the suite shows the test rejects far more often than its nominal level on autocorrelated series without a change. A small p alone is weak evidence here.
- Deduplication matches copies on the second they were sent. If two stored copies of one message carry different timestamp conventions (see the record section), they do not merge, so the months around the change may hold hidden duplicates; not checked.
- The H2 baseline placebo splits the baseline year at a fixed midpoint instead of searching a change week; a searched split could only make its jump larger, so this can only make H2 fail its check more clearly.
- The artifact checks were added after the first real-data run, because two hypotheses passed every pre-registered check yet looked implausibly strong. They are documented as a post-hoc amendment; a reader should weigh them as diagnostics, not as pre-registered tests.
- Rule N2 merges addresses with the same local part under one registered domain, so it can merge two different people who share a local part across an ISP's regional subdomains; a manual check of 50 real merges, sampled from the merges of every rule, found 0 wrong merges (precision 1, exact binomial interval 0.929 to 1); how many were N2 merges is not in the committed check.
- The `other` row of `community_groups.csv` is not a community: it holds the 25838 people with no tie in the baseline period (such as people first seen after it) plus any community of fewer than 5 people. Its ties are published like a group's, but the explorer does not draw it as a group: it labels these people as outside the baseline groups and states, for each week, the share of mail that involves them.
